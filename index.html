Graduate Research Opportunities by Discipline

Students interested in any of the following disciplines are encouraged to apply.

Mechanical Engineering - Dynamic-system modeling, multibody dynamics, mechanical vibration, linear and nonlinear dynamics, and parametric instabilities.

Aerospace Engineering - High-fidelity CFD, aerodynamic loading, aeroelasticity, and flutter analysis.

Electrical Engineering - Modeling and simulation of electric machines, power-electronics converters and control systems, and power-system/grid dynamics.

Civil Engineering - Structural and thermal modeling, lumped-parameter representation, structural dynamics, and base excitation.

Ocean Engineering - Hydrodynamic loads, added mass, wave excitation, and dynamic response of floating structures.

Computational Engineering - Numerical implementation of coupled multiphysics models, numerical methods and algorithm development, high-performance computing, and numerical verification and convergence analysis.

Application Process

Follow the three steps below to pursue an opportunity to join the LPM-FSI Group through the NSF EPSCoR Graduate Fellowship Program.

Already have a qualifying GRFP Honorable Mention? You may begin directly with Step 2.

Step 1

Apply to the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)

Apply to the NSF GRFP for a graduate fellowship. NSF may also award Honorable Mentions to highly rated applicants who are not selected for funding. A qualifying Honorable Mention allows you to apply for the LPM-FSI Group EGFP opportunity in Step 2.

2027 Competition — Fall 2027 Start

Engineering application deadline: October 22, 2026, at 8:00 p.m. Eastern / 7:00 p.m. Central

Recommendation-letter deadline: October 16, 2026, at 8:00 p.m. Eastern / 7:00 p.m. Central

Expected results: Early April 2027

Eligibility
  • Be a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or permanent resident.
  • Plan to pursue a full-time, research-based master's or Ph.D. in an eligible STEM field.
  • Eligible applicants include undergraduate students in their final year, bachelor's degree holders who have not enrolled in a graduate program, and students in the first year of their first graduate degree program.
  • Have never accepted a GRFP fellowship.
  • Not be a current NSF employee.
  • Not hold a master's or doctoral degree.
Required Documents
  • Personal, background, and future-goals statement — maximum 3 pages.
  • Graduate research plan — maximum 2 pages.
  • Academic transcripts.
  • Three recommenders' names and contact information; at least two recommendation letters must be submitted.
  • Education, experience, and proposed field-of-study information in the online application.

Lumped-Parameter Modeling Fluid-Structure Interaction (LPM-FSI)

LPM-FSI is a hybrid analytical–computational framework developed for modeling, simulation, and analysis of distributed-mass systems and their interaction within coupled multiphysics systems.

The framework combines governing-equation-based modeling, computational fluid dynamics, structural dynamics, analytical stability analysis, and high-performance computing to investigate aerodynamic and hydrodynamic loading, thermal effects, vibration, stability, and post-integration behavior, with particular emphasis on expanding analytical stability criteria for integrated multiphysics systems. Applications include renewable energy, offshore infrastructure, and propulsion systems in aerial, naval, and marine vehicles.
